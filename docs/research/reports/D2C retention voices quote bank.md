# Founders blame CAC; repeat economics decide profit

Almost no named Indian D2C leader says acquisition is solved, so the first half of the claim "D2C brands can attract customers; the bigger problem is profitable retention" is not supported as worded. The most common supporting argument actually runs the other way: acquisition is getting more expensive, so profit now has to come from the repeat order. Fixderma's Shaily Mehrotra, Sirona's Deep Bajaj, The Pant Project's Dhruv Toshniwal, Traya's Saloni Anand and Kae Capital's Sunitha Viswanathan all make some version of this point in 2024–2026. The only real founder survey, by DSG Consumer Partners with Meta and ViralMint (Dec 2025, 100+ founders and operators), cuts both ways. Founders rank **rising CAC and unstable ROAS as the single biggest growth blocker**, yet **55% admit to under-investing in CRM and retention** and report **repeat rates of just 10–30%**. Clear counter-voices come from Beco (home care), Honasa (Mamaearth), Bombay Shaving Company, Minimalist and Plum, and Snitch says its CAC has not risen. The discount-waste argument is made in public almost entirely by agencies and vendors. On attribution, **no named Indian operator was found saying CRM or WhatsApp attribution overstates retention**, and the two named global holdout tests found retention messaging mostly incremental. The defensible claim for a Razorpay x ISB Track 2 README is narrower: acquisition is getting costlier and rarely pays back on the first order, so profit depends on repeat purchase, and most brands cannot tell which repeat orders their discounts actually caused. Razorpay sponsors the challenge and appears in two sources in this bank. Meta co-authored the main survey, and several event write-ups are sponsored.

## The case for: rising CAC pushes profit onto the repeat order

The supporting evidence is real, but it rarely says what the claim says. What named in-window leaders actually argue is that **paid acquisition is getting more expensive and its platform metrics no longer show profit, so the economics must be judged on repeat behaviour and payback**. Fixderma now judges acquisition by "contribution margins, repeat behaviour, CAC payback and lifetime value" and warns that ROAS does not show whether growth is "sustainable or profitable" ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/d2c-brands-look-beyond-roas-as-rising-cac-puts-profitability-under-the-lens-157597.html)). Traya runs **separate acquisition and retention P&Ls** and accepts a first-order loss as long as the 12-month cohort pays back ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/mumbai-edition-of-d2cx-converge-decodes-the-%E2%82%B9100-cr-playbook-for-d2c-brands/)). Kae Capital's Viswanathan says the Meta-and-Google arbitrage era depended on cheap capital. Her example of a 3X-ROAS campaign earning roughly 5% margin after discounts and returns is reported as a paraphrase ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/the-end-of-the-roas-era-in-d2c/)). The Souled Store gives the cleanest narrative arc. Its FY22 user base grew while repeat customers leaked, and profit returned only after the founders refocused on product quality, returns and repeat ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/how-d2c-brand-the-souled-store-soared-to-profitability-after-slumping-to-52x-losses/)).

The structural numbers back the "neglected retention" half. The DSGCP survey reports **10–30% repeat rates** and **55% under-investment in CRM and retention** ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/why-most-indian-d2c-brands-fail-to-cross-inr-100-crore-mark/500862)). Unicommerce sets a 90-day repeat floor of 20% ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)), and Forbes India's author puts the survival bar at 35–40% repeat ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/enterprise/startups/indias-d2c-journey-after-a-rapid-scale-up-why-its-now-all-about-discipline/2991796/1)). By any of those standards many brands sit below viability. The same survey found that brands tracking CM2 consistently outperform peers by about **2.3x** ([IndianTelevision](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/three-out-of-four-d2c-brands-now-use-creators-to-drive-sales-says-new-report-251210/)). That supports the "profitable" qualifier specifically. Investors add qualitative weight: Sauce.vc says loyalty is lower ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/from-pre-revenue-to-growth-stage-how-sauce-vcs-thesis-kept-up-with-indias-d2c-boom/)), and Third Eyesight cites a lack of customer stickiness ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/why-most-indian-d2c-brands-fail-to-cross-inr-100-crore-mark/500862)).

Two caveats weaken this camp. First, the brands with the strongest repeat (Plum at 45–50% of website revenue, Kapiva at up to eight purchases a year, Licious with 94% of business from repeat customers) present retention as a **lever that already works, not a problem they cannot solve** ([Medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/i-am-yet-to-come-across-a-dressing-shelf-which-does-not-have-5-or-6-brands-shankar-prasad-plum-goodness/); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/licious-claims-47-yoy-jump-in-fy26-revenue-to-%e2%82%b91166-cr/)). Second, high repeat does not shrink acquisition spend. Plum spent about 37% of FY25 revenue on ads ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/sreekant_interview/is-your-brand-selling-or-are-you-selling-the-brand-plums-shankar-prasad-11073248)) and Honasa about 40% in Q2 FY25 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/mamaearth-honasa-share-price-revenue-distribution-challenges-stock/)). For these brands the problem is less an inability to retain than acquisition spend that never comes down far enough.

## The case against: founders still name acquisition as blocker number one

The strongest counter-evidence is the survey itself. In the only founder survey with a ranking, more respondents named **acquisition efficiency (rising CAC, unstable ROAS)** as their top blocker than funding or category expansion. Over 70% depend on Meta, and 62% report creative fatigue ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/why-most-indian-d2c-brands-fail-to-cross-inr-100-crore-mark/500862); [DSGCP](https://www.dsgcp.com/insights/zero-to-100-cr---marketing-playbook)). Meta co-authored the report and ViralMint is a creative agency, so both have an interest in an acquisition framing. No percentage was published for the ranking either. Even so, it is the clearest primary data available, and it contradicts the claim's first half.

Named counter-voices fall into three patterns. **Demand and salience first**: Honasa's CEO diagnosed Mamaearth's decline as a media, messaging and allocation problem, and answered a repeat-purchase question with Nielsen share ([NSE transcript](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/HONASA16_19022025150003_Transcript.pdf)). Bombay Shaving Company's founder called marketing the hardest part ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shantanudeshpandebsc_one-of-the-hardest-things-about-building-activity-7106121068800585728-mNsW)), Minimalist's CEO said marketing spend is "where most ventures bleed" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/how-d2c-brand-minimalist-built-an-inr-100-cr-business-within-eight-months-of-inception/)), and Plum's CEO ties profitability to brand recall ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/sreekant_interview/is-your-brand-selling-or-are-you-selling-the-brand-plums-shankar-prasad-11073248)). **Category**: Beco's co-founder says flatly that in home care "repeat isn't the problem" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/mumbai-edition-of-d2cx-converge-decodes-the-%E2%82%B9100-cr-playbook-for-d2c-brands/)). Wakefit's older CAC-versus-AOV maths shows that high-ticket, low-frequency categories recover CAC on the first order. **Operations**: The Pant Project ties CAC recovery to a 6% return rate. Unicommerce's platform data puts festive-peak RTO at **39.2%** and shows **58% of COD orders returned versus under 15% for prepaid** ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)). For many Indian merchants, RTO and COD are a bigger and more measurable margin leak than missed repeat orders.

Taken together, the claim holds for replenishable and regimen categories (hair care, skincare, supplements, coffee, meat) and fails for low-frequency or naturally repeating ones. One inference, not sourced: founders may name CAC because it is the visible symptom. Low repeat forces dependence on paid acquisition, which then gets reported as a CAC problem. That reading reconciles the survey's top blocker with its own 10–30% repeat figure, but no source in the bank states it.

## Discount waste and attribution inflation are mostly vendor-voiced claims

On discounts, **named in-window Indian brand leaders talk around the issue rather than admitting it**. WOW's co-founder criticises "short-term growth loops like discounts" ([Medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/consumers-today-dont-lack-ads-they-lack-belief-manish-chowdhary-wow-skin-science/)). Snitch frames markdown pricing as unfair to first buyers ([Indian Retailer](https://indianretailer.com/article/retail-business/fashion-accessories/snitch-didnt-take-birth-studio-it-happened-scrappy)). Neeru's MD is reported as warning against deep discounting ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/how-indian-d2c-brands-are-rewriting-their-playbook-for-profitable-growth/)), and The Souled Store reportedly cut its discount cap from 20% to 7% during its turnaround ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/how-d2c-brand-the-souled-store-soared-to-profitability-after-slumping-to-52x-losses/)). The most useful founder data point is indirect. Plum's repeat share rises from 45–50% to **60–65% during big sales**, which suggests existing customers time their purchases to discounts ([Medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/i-am-yet-to-come-across-a-dressing-shelf-which-does-not-have-5-or-6-brands-shankar-prasad-plum-goodness/)). The explicit "discounts train customers to wait" wording comes only from agencies: Porcellia, Growww Tech, Vamos Digital and Slay Media. All of them sell the shift to retention. Even Porcellia argues for discounts when a second order is probable ([Porcellia](https://www.porcellia.com/blog-posts/indian-d2c-has-developed-discounting-trauma)). No named Indian CRM or retention head was found saying that coupon-led win-backs destroy margin.

On attribution, the gap is starker. **No named Indian operator or vendor leader says CRM, WhatsApp or email attribution overstates retention.** The Indian discussion of broken attribution is about paid media, as with Fixderma on ROAS and Kae Capital's ROAS-versus-margin example. Holdout testing of owned channels appears only in small agency and tool-builder blogs. Globally, the evidence is more nuanced than the popular view that attributed revenue is mostly fake. Klaviyo's own blog asks whether subscribers would have bought without any marketing, but its holdout feature needs 400k+ profiles ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/global-holdout-groups)). Hillstrom's 2025 holdout example found about **92% of email-credited volume incremental** ([MineThatData](https://blog.minethatdata.com/2025/10/what-does-email-mailholdout-test-look.html)), and Jones Road's SMS holdout showed that sending more earned more ([Postscript](https://postscript.io/blog/the-jones-road-test-how-to-measure-and-prove-incrementality-in-sms)). The defensible version is narrow. Klar's Max Rast makes the selection-bias argument: the best customers both use retention tactics more and would buy anyway ([eCom Unity](https://ecomunity.beehiiv.com/p/ecom-unity-3-measuring-retention-with-incrementality-testing)). A 2017 academic study of 70 randomised email-discount experiments found that only about 10% of the extra spend came from redeeming the discount. That points to the discount, not the message, as the wasted margin, though this detail comes from a summary page and is tagged S ([Stanford GSB summary](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/navdeep-sahni-email-promotions-prospective-customers-really-do-work)). Zembula's power maths matters most for mid-size brands. Detecting a 10–20% lift at a 1% baseline needs roughly 40,000–160,000 users per arm, so global holdouts are impractical below about 200k subscribers ([Zembula](https://www.zembula.com/blog/email-holdout-testing-when-it-s-worth-running-and-when-it-s/)). For a Track 2 agent, that argues for **per-decision holdouts and testing discount versus no-discount within the message**, rather than email-versus-no-email.

## Reframe the claim around paid-back repeat, not easy acquisition

The wording "D2C brands can attract customers" should be dropped. The evidence contradicts it, including most of the supporting voices, who complain that acquisition is getting costlier. A version the evidence supports:

> **For Indian D2C brands in repeat-purchase categories, paid acquisition keeps getting more expensive and rarely pays back on the first order, so profit depends on the repeat order. Yet most brands under-invest in retention, see 10–30% repeat rates, and cannot tell which repeat orders their discounts and messages actually caused.**

Each clause has a source. Rising CAC is backed by Toshniwal, Mulani and the survey's top blocker. First-order loss and cohort payback come from Traya and the Villain spokesperson. Under-investment and the 10–30% repeat range come from the DSGCP survey. The final clause, causal measurement, is the README's own argument: it rests on the documented absence of Indian holdout practice and the global selection-bias case, not on a merchant quote. State the scope up front: replenishable categories, not home care or high-ticket items. Also acknowledge that RTO and COD are a parallel margin leak, which a payment-decision agent is well placed to address.

## Conclusion

The public record supports a sharper and more useful problem statement than the original claim. Indian D2C leaders do not say acquisition is easy. They say it no longer pays for itself, and the most sophisticated of them, Traya and Fixderma, already judge it by cohort payback and contribution margin. That moves the opportunity for a Track 2 agent away from "help brands retain" and toward something no named Indian voice yet offers: deciding, customer by customer, whether a discount or payment nudge is needed at all, then proving it with a holdout. Cohort reporting is table stakes for the best operators, while causal measurement of retention offers is absent from the Indian discourse.

Two implications follow. First, the absence of Indian "attribution overstates retention" voices is a positioning gap, not a validated pain. The README should present the incrementality problem as a measurement blind spot the agent exposes, not as something merchants are asking for. Second, the RTO and COD data (58% of COD orders returned versus under 15% for prepaid) puts a payment-decision lever alongside the offer lever. For a Razorpay-sponsored agent, that may be the most defensible margin story in the bank, as long as it is clearly labelled as vendor platform data.
