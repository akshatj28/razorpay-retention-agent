## Q1. Which Indian D2C founders, operators and agency leads make this argument?

It does not get to "prove the incremental lift."

### Inferences
- In India, the "discount trap" argument is now common practitioner wisdom, but it comes almost entirely from **service sellers** (agencies, retention-tool vendors) who benefit from brands moving spend toward retention. Treat it as a motivated, if plausible, consensus.
- The **attribution half of the argument is missing in India.** Nobody I found says "our WhatsApp or CRM dashboard takes credit for orders that would have happened anyway." That gap is itself useful evidence: Indian brands probably are not measuring incrementality on retention at all. Several vendor RFP checklists tell buyers to "distinguish observed results from attributed results" ([CampaignHQ RFP checklist](https://blog.campaignhq.co/retention-automation-rfp-checklist-india-email-whatsapp-2026)), which suggests the problem exists even though no founder states it.
- The strongest Indian "contradicting" voice is Porcellia (IN-1, IN-5). Its position is not "never discount" but "discount only when the second order is probable and the maths works."

### Inferences
- The global evidence is **more nuanced than the "attribution lies" meme**. The two named, published holdout examples I could verify, Hillstrom 2025 and Jones Road/Postscript 2024, both found retention messaging **substantially incremental**. The case against attribution is stronger for **specific flows** (post-purchase, discounted campaigns to loyal customers) than for email or SMS as a whole.
- The robust form of the argument is **selection bias** (Rast, GL-10) together with **customer heterogeneity** (Fader). Your best customers both use retention tools more and would buy anyway, so naive comparisons and attributed revenue overstate the effect. That is defensible. "Most CRM revenue is fake" is not supported by what I found.
- The "impractical for small brands" counter (Zembula; Klaviyo's 400k-profile minimum) bears directly on India, where most D2C brands have lists far below 200k. That makes cheaper alternatives more relevant than full global holdouts: per-flow holdouts, long-duration small holdouts, or decision-level control groups.

## Q3. What published data shows how much "attributed" retention revenue is actually incremental?

### Takeaway
**Reliable published numbers are scarce, and they conflict.** No ESP (Klaviyo, Postscript, Attentive) publishes an aggregate "X% of attributed revenue is incremental" figure. The best evidence is (a) academic field experiments showing promotional email lifts spend well beyond redemptions, (b) single-brand holdout case studies, which mostly show positive lift, and (c) vendor and agency claims that attributed revenue exceeds holdout-measured lift, usually without disclosed methods.

### Cited Findings
- **Academic, 70 randomised email-discount experiments** (Sahni, Zou and Chintagunta, *Management Science* 2017; data from 2009–2011 on an online ticket-resale platform). Recipients of email promotions spent **$3.03 more (+37%)** than opted-in non-recipients. **Only about 10% of the extra spend came from redeeming the discount**, a large share went to categories the offer did not cover, and spending stayed about 25% higher the week after the offer expired. The finding cuts both ways. Redemption-based ROI *understates* the email's effect, but it also means **the discount itself was not doing most of the work**; the reminder or advertising effect was. That supports "the discount is wasted margin" more than "the email is fake." — [INFORMS listing](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2450); [Stanford GSB summary](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/navdeep-sahni-email-promotions-prospective-customers-really-do-work) **[S]**. Older than the 2022–26 window but foundational.
- **Hillstrom 2025:** about 92% of email-credited volume was incremental in his illustrative example; email drives about 15–20% of annual volume for clients. — [MineThatData](https://blog.minethatdata.com/2025/10/what-does-email-mailholdout-test-look.html). Chart-dependent; verify.
- **Jones Road SMS (Postscript, 2024):** in a one-month random split (50% standard, 25% holdout, 25% at 2x frequency), the holdout spent the least. Incremental ROI of standard vs holdout was 11%. The 2x group showed about a 10% revenue-per-subscriber lift over standard (98% certainty) and about $0.33 of incremental revenue per subscriber. — [Postscript](https://postscript.io/blog/the-jones-road-test-how-to-measure-and-prove-incrementality-in-sms)
- **AskBiz (vendor blog) case:** a 10% holdout produced estimated incremental revenue of SGD 9,800, **below** Klaviyo's attributed figure. The author says post-purchase flows often show very low incrementality, while abandoned cart, browse abandonment and win-back flows consistently show real lift. — [AskBiz](https://askbiz.co/blog/email-marketing-roi-klaviyo-pos-integration) **[S]**. Small and vendor-authored.
- **Attribution window mechanics:** if someone on your list buys anything within 5 days of clicking any email, Klaviyo attributes the sale to email. That structurally credits organic repeat orders. — [Get Creative, "Your Klaviyo Dashboard Is Lying to You"](https://get-creative.co/blog/how-to-measure-email-roi-without-inflated-attribution-numbers-because-your-klaviyo-dashboard-is-lying-to-you) **[S]**
- **Klaviyo 2026 benchmark:** automated flows produced about 41% of email revenue from 5.3% of sends. That is *attributed*, not incremental. — reported in the [Klaviyo analytics write-up](https://improvado.io/blog/klaviyo-analytics) **[S]**
- **Example of an attributed claim:** a Cinch case study claims **$368,534 attributed** revenue from a $1,685 SMS spend, with no holdout. This is the kind of figure the sceptics object to. — [Cinch](https://content.cinch.io/amplifying-retention-how-sms-added-368k-in-incremental-revenue-in-30-days/)
- **SMS attributed share:** a vendor guide says mature SMS programmes commonly report 5–20% of total revenue as SMS-attributed, and warns that last-click inflates this. — [Zembula / SMS guide context](https://www.zembula.com/blog/email-holdout-testing-when-it-s-worth-running-and-when-it-s/) **[S]**; source not independently verified.
- **Unusable:** Admetrics' "Incrementality Email Marketing Statistics 2026" figures (an 18–26% lift, 320% more incremental revenue from flows, and others) have **no attributed source or methodology**. Exclude them. — [Admetrics](https://www.admetrics.io/en/post/incrementality-email-marketing-statistics-dtc-ecommerce-17239)
- **Academic, retention targeting** (from memory, not verified this session): Eva Ascarza, "Retention Futility: Targeting High-Risk Customers Might Be Ineffective," *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2018. Field experiments found that the customers most likely to churn are not necessarily the ones whose behaviour a retention offer changes, which argues for uplift-based targeting. **Verify before citing**; listed here as a lead.

### Inferences
- The defensible, data-backed version of the sub-claim is **narrow**. (1) Discounts inside retention messages are often not the active ingredient (Sahni et al.), so blanket codes to existing customers give away margin. (2) Attribution windows credit organic repeat orders, especially on post-purchase and replenishment flows. (3) Lift varies so much by brand and flow that only your own holdout tells you the answer (Haus). The broad version ("most retention revenue is not incremental") has **no solid published support**, and two named case studies contradict it.
- For a decision-agent product, the published research points to "**test discount vs no-discount within the retention message**" rather than "email vs no email" as the higher-value, lower-sample-size experiment.

### Gaps
- No aggregate holdout benchmark across many brands from Klaviyo, Postscript, Attentive, Haus, Measured or Recast was found.
- No Indian holdout result for WhatsApp, email or SMS retention was found.
- The Sahni et al. and Ascarza details come from summaries and memory, not full text.

## Q4. Is there data on discount-trained customers or promotion dependence in India?

### Takeaway
**Survey data on Indian shoppers deferring purchases until sales is thin and indirect.** The evidence is festive-season studies showing discounts as the top purchase trigger for about half of urban shoppers, research timed around sale windows, and unsourced agency claims that sale-acquired customers repeat less. I found **no rigorous Indian dataset** comparing the LTV or repeat rates of sale-event-only buyers with full-price buyers.

### Cited Findings
- **Saarva festive study (Sep 2026, n=1,006 urban consumers):** discounts were the biggest purchase trigger, cited by **49%**. Shopper mindsets: Price Comparers 52%, Early Planners 36%, Budget Stretchers 15%, Impulse Buyers 13%. — [Business Standard / ANI press release](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/content/press-releases-ani/urban-indians-set-to-spend-more-this-festive-season-but-on-fewer-things-saarva-study-126100100639_1.html) **[S]**. A press release, so possibly sponsored.
- **Datum Intelligence Festive Barometer 2026:** 36% of Diwali shoppers were researching products by 1 Aug, 53% by 1 Sep and **70% by 1 Oct**, ahead of sale windows opening around 9–10 Oct. This suggests deliberate research followed by buying at the sale, but the study does not report a deferral rate. — [BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/insights/seven-in-10-festive-shoppers-start-looking-before-sales-open-are-brands-advertising-too-late-12570112) **[S]**
- **Deloitte India (2020, pandemic-era):** 55% of Indian consumers were "scouting for better deals and bargains"; 43% would wait for better deals or buy used, for vehicles. — [Deloitte India press release](https://www.deloitte.com/in/en/about/press-room/indian-consumers-join-in-festivities-with-a-higher-shopping-intensity.html) **[S]**. ### Inferences
- The India evidence supports "**Indian shoppers are highly discount-responsive and plan around sale events**" (about half name discounts as the main trigger, and research peaks before sale windows). It does **not** yet support a quantified "discount-trained customers have X% lower LTV" claim. Any such figure in a deliverable should be framed as practitioner opinion, or as a hypothesis the product would measure.
- The low Indian list sizes implied by D2C scale, together with the 400k-profile threshold for Klaviyo-style global holdouts, suggest a product angle: lightweight, per-decision holdouts. That is my inference, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No Myntra EORS, Amazon GIF, Flipkart BBD or Nykaa Pink Friday data on sale-only buyer cohorts (share of customers who buy only during sales, or their LTV) was found in public sources.
- No Redseer or Bain India figure on promotion dependence of D2C repeat buyers was found. I did not search deeply for one; it is worth a targeted search.
- The DSG Consumer Partners "55% under-invest in CRM" claim was not verified at the source.
