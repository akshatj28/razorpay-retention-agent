# Indian beauty, personal-care and skincare D2C leaders on acquisition vs. retention (2023–2026): quote bank

Claim being tested: **"D2C brands can attract customers; the bigger problem is profitable retention."**

**How each quote was checked** (this tag appears on every record):
- **[V-raw]**: I read the page's raw text myself (downloaded HTML or the NSE PDF transcript). The wording matches the page character for character.
- **[V-fetch]**: An automated fetch tool pulled out the exact words from the page. That tool is a summarizer. I asked it for exact words but could not check them character by character, so re-check against the URL before using a [V-fetch] quote in a final deliverable.
- **[P]**: Paraphrase or reporter narration. These are not the speaker's own words, so do not put them in quotation marks as theirs.
- **[S]**: Seen only in a search-result snippet or search summary. Not verified on the page.

Verdict scale, against the claim: **supports / partly supports / contradicts / neutral**.

Source-type flags: **[peer]** means not a beauty or personal-care brand; it is included only as a comparator. **[unnamed]** means a company spokesperson, not a named person. **[vendor/agency]** means the speaker sells marketing services. Neither exchange4media D2C roundup I used carries a "sponsored" label, but both are composite features. Brands appear to have supplied their quotes on request, and some are credited only to "a spokesperson".

## Q1. Which named leaders say acquisition is getting too expensive and retention or repeat is the real lever or challenge?

### Takeaway
The clearest supporting voices on record are from 2024–2026. Shaily Mehrotra (Fixderma) and Deep Bajaj (Sirona) say rising or competitive acquisition costs push the economics onto repeat behaviour and LTV. Brands like Foxtale, Kapiva, Traya and Plum show their repeat share with pride. They present retention as the engine they already have working, **not** as the problem they can't solve. Almost no named beauty founder says outright, on record, that "retention is our biggest problem". The "supports" evidence mostly reads as "CAC is rising, so retention must carry the economics."

### Inferences
- In this category, the "supports" camp mostly argues "CAC is rising, so judge acquisition by payback and LTV". Fixderma, Sirona and Villain fit this. Their stated problem is the cost of the first order. Retention is the fix they name, not a failure they report. The literal claim ("can attract customers; retention is the bigger problem") is backed only **partly** by founder statements.
- Brands with strong efficacy or "regimen" positioning frame repeat purchase as already solved. These are Minimalist, Foxtale, Kapiva, Traya and Fixderma. Even so, some still spend heavily on marketing: Kapiva's marketing spend is INR 188 Cr against INR 342 Cr of FY25 business (see Q4 table). High repeat does not remove acquisition spend.
- Prasad says repeat share jumps to 60–65% during big sales, and that repeat is unmeasurable off-site. Both point to the gap a retention or repeat-decision tool would fill: repeat that depends on sales events, and no visibility on marketplaces. This is an inference, not his claim.

### Gaps
- I found no named Honasa/Mamaearth, SUGAR, mCaffeine, Dot & Key, Bare Anatomy/Innovist, Juicy Chemistry, The Derma Co or Mosaic Wellness (Revant Bhate) quote stating retention is the bigger problem.
- Anurag Kedia (Pilgrim) on Maharajas of Scale: [S] A search summary says he described cohorts as healthy and Pilgrim as acquiring many more new customers than repeat. The episode page failed to load (SSL error), so this is unverified ([source](https://www.maharajasofscale.com/blog/episode/s2-e7-winning-with-a-d2c-brand-anurag-kedia-of-pilgrim-cosmetics/)).
- Tarun Sharma (mCaffeine): a search snippet showed a LinkedIn-style passage: "When CAC rises that hard and you cannot recover it in pricing or retention, margins compress." I could not confirm the author, date or URL, so it is **excluded** as unverified.

## Q2. Who has said discount-led growth or retention hurt margins, or trained customers to wait for discounts?

### Takeaway
Thin. No named Indian beauty founder I could verify says discounts "trained" customers to wait. The closest on-record signals:
- Manish Chowdhary (WOW) criticises "short-term growth loops like discounts".
- Plum's Shankar Prasad says repeat-customer revenue share rises to 60–65% during big sales. This is indirect evidence that repeat clusters around sale events.
- Mohit Yadav (Minimalist) refuses to compete on lowest price. Minimalist's no-flash-sale discipline is documented only by third parties.

### Inferences
- Founders on record rarely blame discounting directly. Two reasons are likely: it implicates their own playbook, and marketplace or quick-commerce discounting is set partly by platforms. Wording about discounts "training" customers comes from consultancies and agency blogs, not from founders. Examples are [Porcellia](https://www.porcellia.com/blog-posts/indian-d2c-has-developed-discounting-trauma) [vendor/agency] and a [PM case study](https://www.mypminterview.com/p/case-study-why-indian-beauty-d2c-brands-fail). Do not attribute it to founders.
- Plum's sale-time repeat jump of 45–50% → 60–65% is the most quotable founder-sourced data point that existing customers time their purchases to sales.

### Gaps
- No verified founder quote uses "trained", "wait for sale" or "discount-seekers" for an Indian beauty brand.
- SUGAR's discounting and margin story rests on third-party analysis. FashionNetwork and Business Standard pages were blocked or paywalled.

## Q3. Who says acquisition, CAC or brand demand is still the main problem? (contradicting voices)

### Takeaway
Several heavyweight voices frame the hard problem as **getting demand and trial**: brand salience, media allocation, marketing spend as a share of revenue. They do not frame it as keeping customers.
- **Varun Alagh (Honasa)**: on the Q3 FY25 call he treated Mamaearth's decline as a media and messaging problem. He answered a repeat-purchase question with Nielsen share and distributor data.
- **Shantanu Deshpande (Bombay Shaving Company)**: said marketing, meaning trial and salience, was the hardest part.
- **Mohit Yadav (Minimalist)**: said marketing spend is "where most ventures bleed".
- **Shankar Prasad (Plum)**: said rising CAC is structural but he had "not noticed a dramatic increase" in CPA. He says profitability comes from brand recall.

### Inferences

Asked about consumer repeat purchase, it answers with distributor reorders and Nielsen share. Two readings are possible:

## Q4. What repeat-rate, LTV, CAC and marketing-spend numbers have they disclosed?

### Takeaway
Repeat numbers are disclosed far more often than CAC. **No named beauty founder disclosed an absolute CAC figure** in the sources I could verify. Marketing intensity runs from **~25–28%** (Minimalist) to **~40% or more** (Honasa Q2 FY25, Kapiva).

### Inferences
- Disclosed marketing intensity is high even for brands with strong repeat: Plum ~37% of revenue on ads in FY25, Honasa ~40% in Q2 FY25, Mosaic ~41% in FY24. That fits a mixed reading. Repeat is real, but acquisition spend still eats the P&L. The issue is less "can't acquire" and more "acquisition spend never comes down enough".
- Every repeat figure is a share of revenue or users, on the brand's own site where stated. None is a cohort second-order rate. Prasad says marketplace repeat is unmeasurable. The first-to-second-order conversion rate, the metric most relevant to the claim, is **not publicly disclosed by any named brand** I found.

### Gaps
- No verified absolute CAC, CAC payback or LTV:CAC from any named beauty or personal-care founder. Secondary blogs cite figures, such as Pilgrim LTV:CAC ~3:1 on a business-model template site, but those are unsourced and excluded.
- No second-order (first-to-second purchase) conversion rate disclosed by any named brand.
- Honasa repeat or retention KPIs: the DRHP/RHP were not read (BSE PDF not opened). That remains a possible primary source.
