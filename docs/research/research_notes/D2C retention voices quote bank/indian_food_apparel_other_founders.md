# Indian non-beauty D2C leaders on acquisition vs. retention: quote bank (food/bev, nutrition, apparel, footwear, home, other)

Claim tested: "D2C brands can attract customers; the bigger problem is profitable retention."

Verdict key: **S** = supports, **PS** = partly supports, **C** = contradicts, **N** = neutral/tangential.

Method note (read first): Quotes below came from pages I fetched and read. My page-reading tool pulls text through a summarising model, so wording is very likely exact but should be spot-checked against the URL before it goes on a slide. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** appeared only in search-result summaries. I did not open the page itself, so treat them as leads and not as quotes. **[PARAPHRASE]** means the source gives no direct quote. **[VENDOR/SPONSORED]** marks event-partner, Brand Connect, agency or SaaS-vendor content. Older items (pre-2023) are marked **[OLDER]**.

## Q1. Which named leaders say acquisition is getting expensive and retention/repeat is the real challenge?

### Takeaway
Few named non-beauty Indian D2C leaders say outright "acquisition is easy, retention is the problem". The two strongest on-record voices are Vedang Patel (The Souled Store, 2023) and Matt Chitharanjan (Blue Tokai, 2022, older). Patel lived the pattern: his user base grew while repeat customers fell away. Chitharanjan advised putting retention ahead of acquisition because CAC had risen. Most other "retention matters" lines come from 2022 founder Q&As, from vendors, or from event write-ups with no attributed quotes.

### Inferences

- The only on-record "retention rather than acquisition" recommendation (Blue Tokai) dates from 2022, a post-COVID CAC spike. It should be presented as older.
- In this sample, "retention is the growth engine" lines are much more common in sponsored or event-narration copy than in quotation-marked statements by named brand leaders. That gap is itself worth reporting.

## Q2. Who said discounts or sales hurt margin, or trained customers to wait?

### Takeaway
Only two attributable data points surfaced. Siddharth Dungarwal (Snitch) criticises markdown-led pricing because it overcharges first buyers. The Souled Store cut discounts from up to 20% to a 7% cap during its profitability turnaround. I found no named Indian non-beauty brand leader saying sales "trained customers to wait". That framing appears only in vendor and consultant blogs.

### Inferences
- Snitch's critique targets the *legacy markdown model* (EOSS-style inflated MRP). It is not a complaint about its own discount dependence. It supports "discounts are a margin/trust problem" but is offered as a differentiator, not an admission.
- Souled Store's discount cut, together with the return to profitability, is the most concrete Indian non-beauty evidence that discount dependence was hurting margin. It is reported by Inc42, not stated in a direct quote.

### Gaps

This needs targeted podcast or LinkedIn review (e.g. Bewakoof's turnaround under new ownership, Bombay Shirt Company, Rare Rabbit's no-discount positioning). I could not verify any of these.
- No named-leader quote was found linking COD/RTO costs to repeat-order margin.

**Survey evidence (contradicting, with caveats)**
- DSG Consumer Partners × Meta × Viralmint report, survey of 100+ Indian D2C founders and operators, as reported by Entrepreneur India on 15 Dec 2025. Acquisition efficiency (rising CAC, unstable ROAS) was the single biggest growth blocker, cited by more founders than funding or category expansion. Over 70% use Meta as their primary acquisition channel. 62% say repeated creatives stop sustaining ROAS despite higher spend. The same survey also reports **55% of founders admit under-investing in CRM/retention** and repeat purchase rates of **10–30%**. About 60–65% of brands stay in the INR 1–50 Cr band. **[VENDOR CO-AUTHORED: Meta is a report partner]**. Pooja Shirali, VP at DSG Consumer Partners (an investor, not a brand), is quoted: "brands that master Meta's ecosystem…" (fragment). — [Entrepreneur India, 15 Dec 2025](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/why-most-indian-d2c-brands-fail-to-cross-inr-100-crore-mark/500862)

### Inferences
- The DSG/Meta survey cuts both ways. Founders *name* acquisition as their top pain, yet most admit under-investing in retention and report weak repeat (10–30%). One reading: founders feel acquisition pain most, while the under-addressed lever is retention. For the challenge narrative, that is a "partly supports" reading. The stated #1 blocker still contradicts the claim as written.
- Contradicting voices cluster in two patterns. (a) Brands with strong organic or brand pull (Snitch) report flat CAC. (b) Brands whose bottleneck is marketplace or offline reach (boAt, Rage Coffee) treat it as a channel or distribution problem. Neither is a retention story.
- Wakefit's 2019 quote shows category matters. In high-AOV, low-frequency categories (mattresses, furniture), the first order pays back CAC on its own, so retention is structurally less central. Treat this as a scope caveat for any "retention is the bigger problem" claim.

### Gaps
- I could not open the boAt exchange4media article (403), so only the headline is verified. The fuller transcript is needed.
- No 2023–2026 contradicting quote from a food, nutrition or footwear founder was found beyond Snitch (apparel).
- Search summaries mention Wakefit's Q4 FY26 results noting "increased investments in advertising and marketing… heightened competitive intensity" and marketing at about 7.3% of revenue **[UNVERIFIED, not fetched]**: [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/wakefit-q4-pat-skyrockets-to-₹122-cr-revenue-up-14-yoy/). This would be a company-filing data point, not a founder quote.

### Inferences
- The brands with the highest disclosed repeat (Licious at 94% of business, Souled Store at 130%/yr) are the ones whose leaders talk *least* about acquisition pain. That is consistent with the claim: once retention is solved, acquisition becomes manageable. But both are high-frequency or fandom categories and may not generalise.
- Repeat-rate definitions differ widely: share of revenue, six-month repeat, annual orders per customer (>100%), "lifetime repeat". Don't compare these numbers across brands without normalising.
- No named non-beauty Indian leader in this sample disclosed an LTV/CAC ratio. Investor-guide benchmarks (e.g. LTV:CAC ≥3:1) appear only in vendor blogs and were not included as evidence.

### Gaps
- No LTV/CAC ratio or second-order rate (first→second purchase conversion) disclosed by a named non-beauty brand was found.
- Lenskart (listed; per search summaries, about nine months before mid-2026), Wakefit (listed) and boAt (IPO filing) would have repeat or CAC disclosures in their DRHP/RHP or investor presentations. I did not retrieve these. They are the most reliable next source.
- I could not verify Two Brothers Organic Farms' reported 60% of revenue from its own website/app (Oct 2025, attributed to Economic Times in a summary). It is a channel metric, not a repeat metric.
