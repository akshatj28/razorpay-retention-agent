# Indian consumer/D2C investors and research firms on CAC vs profitable retention (2023–2026)

Claim being tested: "D2C brands can attract customers; the bigger problem is profitable retention."
Verdict key: SUPPORTS / PARTLY SUPPORTS / CONTRADICTS. "Verbatim" means the exact words appeared in the fetched page. "Paraphrase" means it did not, or the wording was the reporter's. Several fetches returned text as summarised by a fetch tool. Wording marked verbatim was returned as a direct quote, but re-check it against the live page before publishing.

Research date: 13 Oct 2026. About 35 search and fetch calls. exchange4media pages returned HTTP 403, so they could not be read.

## Q1. What have named consumer-VC partners said about CAC inflation, Meta dependence, repeat rates, LTV and discount-led growth?

### Takeaway
I found very few on-record, in-window (2023–2026) quotes from named partners at the listed funds that address CAC versus retention head-on. Most partner commentary is about brand building, quick commerce or fund returns. The most citable investor-side material is institutional: the DSGCP-led playbook (Dec 2025) and an Elevation Capital case study (2021, outside the window). The firm-level views that exist mostly frame the problem as both acquiring and retaining efficiently, not as retention alone.

## Q2. What do founder surveys and reports say about top growth blockers and retention?

### Takeaway
The main primary survey, by DSGCP with Meta and ViralMint (Dec 2025, 100+ founders and operators), reportedly names acquisition efficiency (rising CAC and unstable ROAS) as the single biggest growth blocker. It also finds 55% of brands under-invest in CRM and retention, and repeat rates of only 10–30%. The industry diagnosis is therefore "acquisition is the felt pain; retention is the neglected fix." That partly contradicts the claim as framed.

The claim holds better as "the under-addressed problem is profitable retention" than as "acquisition is solved."
- The CM2 2.3x finding and the discounting admission back the "profitable" qualifier specifically. The issue is not just repeat, but repeat that is not bought with discounts.

- I could not access the full DSGCP PDF. The "single biggest blocker" ranking has no published percentage, and the 10–30% repeat range has no stated time window (90-day vs annual).

## Q3. Who argues acquisition/CAC is still the dominant blocker?

### Takeaway
Contradicting evidence is real and comes from three places: the DSGCP/Meta founder survey (acquisition efficiency is the #1 blocker), consultants who call online scaling "capital-hungry in terms of CAC", and founders in high-repeat categories who say repeat is not their problem.

### Cited Findings
- DSGCP × Meta × ViralMint survey: "acquisition efficiency, rising CAC and unstable ROAS, is the single biggest blocker to growth". Verdict: CONTRADICTS. — [Entrepreneur India, 15 Dec 2025](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/why-most-indian-d2c-brands-fail-to-cross-inr-100-crore-mark/500862)
- 62% cite creative fatigue, an acquisition-side bottleneck. Over 70% depend on Meta. Verdict: CONTRADICTS. — [IndianTelevision](https://indiantelevision.com/mam/three-out-of-four-d2c-brands-now-use-creators-to-drive-sales-says-new-report-251210/)
- Aditya Ruia, Cofounder, Beco (home care D2C), 9 Apr 2026. Verbatim: "In categories like home care, repeat isn't the problem – brand engagement and acquisition is." Source type: event coverage, Inc42. Verdict: CONTRADICTS. This is the clearest direct counter-quote, and it shows the claim is category-dependent. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/mumbai-edition-of-d2cx-converge-decodes-the-%E2%82%B9100-cr-playbook-for-d2c-brands/)
- Devangshu Dutta, Third Eyesight. Verbatim: "Scaling up online can be very rapid, but is also capital-hungry in terms of CAC." Verdict: CONTRADICTS. — [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/why-most-indian-d2c-brands-fail-to-cross-inr-100-crore-mark/500862)
- Kanwaljit Singh, Fireside: rising quick-commerce marketing costs are "a real challenge" (7 Jan 2026). Verdict: PARTLY CONTRADICTS. Acquisition cost pressure is migrating to quick commerce. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/interviews/we-have-7080-success-because-we-understand-the-consumer-playbook-firesides-kanwaljit-singh)
- Founder voices that link the two. Saloni Anand, Cofounder, Traya Health, 9 Apr 2026. Verbatim: "we look at acquisition P&L and retention P&L separately, and we're okay losing money on the first order as long as the 12-month cohort turns profitable". Also verbatim: "We defined growth backwards from LTV." Verdict: SUPPORTS, because profitability is decided by the retention cohort, not the first order. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/mumbai-edition-of-d2cx-converge-decodes-the-%E2%82%B9100-cr-playbook-for-d2c-brands/)
- Udit Toshniwal, Founder, The Pant Project, 9 Apr 2026. Verbatim: "We operate at 6% returns versus an industry average of 25-30%, which directly impacts CAC recovery and profitability". Verdict: PARTLY SUPPORTS. Post-purchase leakage (returns, RTO) erodes CAC recovery. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/mumbai-edition-of-d2cx-converge-decodes-the-%E2%82%B9100-cr-playbook-for-d2c-brands/)
- Snitch's CMO (per StartupTalky, paraphrase): retail was an offensive bet from day one, not a defensive move against CAC. Verdict: weakly CONTRADICTS the "offline = CAC escape" narrative. — [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/india-d2c-brands-opening-physical-stores/)

### Inferences
- The claim is category-sensitive. In consumables and regimen categories (hair care, supplements, beauty), retention is the profit lever. In low-engagement, high-natural-repeat categories (home care), acquisition and brand salience bind first.
- Survey respondents' top-blocker answer (CAC/ROAS) may reflect the symptom. Low repeat (10–30%) forces dependence on paid acquisition, which then shows up as "CAC is the problem." This is an inference, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- I found no in-window named VC partner arguing on the record that acquisition, not retention, is the dominant blocker.

## Q4. Concrete numbers: CAC trends, repeat rates, share of revenue from repeat, payback

### Takeaway
The numbers are directional and mostly vendor-sourced. Credible sources converge on: repeat rates of 10–30% (DSGCP survey), a 20% 90-day repeat floor (Unicommerce, normative), Meta/Google CAC inflation of roughly 25–40% over 2–3 years (vendor and analyst estimates), and investor heuristics of LTV:CAC ≥3 and payback under 6 months.

### Cited Findings
- Repeat rates of 10–30% (DSGCP × Meta × ViralMint survey, Dec 2025). — [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/why-most-indian-d2c-brands-fail-to-cross-inr-100-crore-mark/500862)
- A 90-day repeat rate below 20% means "you do not have a D2C brand" (normative, Unicommerce 2026). — [Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)
- Repeat purchase rates must exceed 35–40% to endure (Forbes India author statement, Feb 2026, unsourced). — [Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/enterprise/startups/indias-d2c-journey-after-a-rapid-scale-up-why-its-now-all-about-discipline/2991796/1)
- Early-stage D2C brands spent 30–40% of revenue on digital ads. Marketing budgets were cut 25–40% in 2022–24. — [Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/enterprise/startups/indias-d2c-journey-after-a-rapid-scale-up-why-its-now-all-about-discipline/2991796/1)
- Ad inflation: Google Ads CPC rose 30–100% across categories in 2024 and Facebook CPM rose 25–40%, putting CAC at INR 800–1,200 per new customer. Source type: market-research vendor (Mordor), so treat with caution. — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-d2c-ecommerce-market)
- Meta/Google CAC up 25–40% year-on-year for Indian D2C (StartupTalky estimate). — [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/india-d2c-brands-opening-physical-stores/)
- Meta CAC up roughly 40% over two years (agency blog). — [Aghor Labs](https://aghorlabs.com/blog/the-ultimate-d2c-playbook-for-india-in-2025-building-profitable-consumer-brands)
- Average 30-day repeat purchase is 12% and Meta CAC is up 32% year-on-year (single-author founder blog, 200+ brands, unaudited). — [Growwwtech](https://growwwtech.com/blog/state-of-indian-d2c-2026-industry-report)
- About 20% of first-time Indian D2C buyers return within three months (CX vendor blog, unsourced). — [Genesys](https://www.genesys.com/en-sg/blog/post/indias-d2c-cx-reckoning)
- Investor heuristics: 3-year LTV/CAC of 3 or more is "great, irrespective of category" (Elevation, 2021). ### Inferences
- The 10–30% repeat band (DSGCP) against the 20% 90-day floor (Unicommerce) and the 35–40% survival bar (Forbes India) implies many brands fall below what analysts consider viable. That supports retention as a structural weakness. Ad inflation of 25–40% over 2–3 years shows acquisition is getting harder too, so the evidence supports "both are worsening, and retention is the neglected half."
