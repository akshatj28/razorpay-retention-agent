# Indian D2C operators and ecosystem vendors on acquisition vs profitable retention (2023-2026): quote bank

Claim being tested in every verdict: **"D2C brands can attract customers; the bigger problem is profitable retention."**
Verdict scale: Supports / Partly supports / Contradicts.

How to read these records:
- **Verbatim status.** "Verbatim" means I saw the wording on the fetched source page. Because the fetch tool passes pages through a summariser, a few words may differ from the original. Spot-check any quote on the live URL before it goes into a final deliverable.
- **Long quotes.** Quotes over 25 words are split into fragments.
- **Paraphrases.** Each one is marked **[PARAPHRASE]** and must not be presented as a quote.
- **Vendor bias.** "Vendor" means the speaker's company sells retention, checkout, CRM, logistics or marketing tooling and has an interest in the claim. Two records are Razorpay/BiteSpeed press-release lines, and Razorpay sponsors this challenge, so treat them as the most interested sources in the bank.
- **Dates.** Several of the strongest quotes are pre-2023 (marked **[PRE-WINDOW]**). They are kept because the question asks for supporting and contradicting views and 2023-2026 named material is thin.

## Q1. Who says Indian D2C brands can acquire customers but struggle to retain them, or that the second order or repeat rate is the real problem?

### Takeaway
Named operators back the claim when they look back on burn-era mistakes (The Souled Store) or design around cohort payback (Traya). The 2026 press consensus (Inc42 "D2C 3.0", exchange4media) also treats low repeat rates as a structural failure mode. However, operators in low-frequency categories (Beco, home care) and ops-focused founders (The Pant Project, on returns) push back. They say the binding constraint is acquisition or engagement, or operations, not retention.

- Vendor voices (BiteSpeed, Razorpay, Pickrr/Shiprocket, Wigzo) uniformly support the claim. That is expected and should carry little evidentiary weight.

## Q2. Who says discount- or coupon-led retention destroys margin, or that "retention" campaigns mostly reach customers who would have bought anyway?

### Takeaway
The clearest named statement of the "would have bought anyway" problem is Malika Sadani (The Moms Co). It is from 2020, outside the window. In-window named support is indirect: Kae Capital's 3X ROAS vs 5% margin example (which includes discounts), Neeru's discount and gross-margin discipline, and The Souled Store's pricing fight with marketplaces. Most explicit anti-discount content found is vendor or agency blogs, not named operators.

### Cited Findings
- **Malika Sadani, The Moms Co [PRE-WINDOW].** Brands keep acquiring and then pay to make customers repeat "without checking how many of them would come back organically without any nudge." She adds that this matters "to know whether the consumer really likes the product you offer". Not a vendor. Verdict: Supports. — [Inc42, 31 Jul 2020](https://inc42.com/features/how-d2c-brands-are-leveraging-martech-solutions-social-media-to-solve-the-retention-puzzle/)
- **Sunitha Viswanathan, Kae Capital [PARAPHRASE].** Example of a 3X ROAS campaign with about 5% margin after COGS, discounts and returns. Verdict: Partly supports. — [Inc42, 21 Jul 2026](https://inc42.com/features/the-end-of-the-roas-era-in-d2c/)
- **Avnish Kumar, Neeru's.** Quote: "It's better to make a product for your sale days…". Reporter paraphrase: he warned against deep discounting and stressed protecting gross margins. Verdict: Partly supports. — [Inc42, 19 Aug 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/how-indian-d2c-brands-are-rewriting-their-playbook-for-profitable-growth/)
- **Vedang Patel, The Souled Store.** "We kept increasing the prices, and marketplaces kept decreasing them." This is about channel discounting eroding pricing. Verdict: Partly supports. — [Inc42, 22 May 2023](https://inc42.com/startups/how-d2c-brand-the-souled-store-soared-to-profitability-after-slumping-to-52x-losses/)
- **Littlebox (Alok Paul, COO) [PARAPHRASE].** Relies on discounts to clear excess inventory. This shows discounting used as an inventory tool, not a retention tool. — [Inc42, 8 Sep 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/how-can-d2c-founders-scale-business-without-losing-control)
- **Agency and vendor blogs (no named operators; vendor-interested).**
  - Porcellia argues Indian D2C has "discounting trauma". It says founders swung from "Discount as much as possible" to never discounting, and that each discount should be judged by its effect on CM2. This is a contrarian, pro-selective-discount view. — [Porcellia](https://www.porcellia.com/blog-posts/indian-d2c-has-developed-discounting-trauma)
  - Supermanager claims the 90-day repeat rate of sale-acquired customers is 20-35% lower. The study is unnamed and the figure unverified. — [supermanager.co](https://www.supermanager.co/blog/why-discounts-are-killing-your-d2c-brand)
  - WTF Amplify notes that loyal customers may reorder without any campaign, and that last-message attribution overstates the agency's contribution. — [WTF Amplify playbook](https://wtfamplify.com/playbooks/d2c-retention-marketing-agency-india)

### Inferences
- The "would have bought anyway" argument is recognised by at least one respected founder and by agencies. In 2023-2026 Indian press it is rarely stated by named operators. That suggests either an under-articulated pain or one practitioners don't voice publicly. Either way it is a differentiating angle, but evidence for it as a felt merchant pain is thin.
- Discounting in the in-window evidence is discussed mainly as an acquisition, sale-event or inventory lever, not as a retention lever.

### Gaps
- No named 2023-2026 Indian CRM or retention head was found saying coupon-led win-back campaigns destroy margin.
- No named Indian loyalty-vendor leader (Nector, Easy Rewardz, Swym) quote on discounts vs loyalty was found within budget.

## Q3. Who says attribution in CRM, WhatsApp or email tools overstates retention results, or argues for holdout or incrementality testing?

### Takeaway
I found no named Indian operator or vendor leader explicitly saying CRM or WhatsApp attribution overstates retention. The evidence is (a) editorial and unnamed-marketer commentary on broken attribution, (b) an investor's ROAS-vs-margin example, and (c) agency and vendor blogs recommending random holdouts for WhatsApp broadcasts. The topic is discussed mostly for paid media, not for owned-channel retention.

### Cited Findings
- **Unnamed senior marketer.** "Attribution is no longer about finding a single answer; it is about reducing the number of wrong ones." The article narrates Indian marketers "running geo holdout tests, audience split experiments and media blackout studies to measure true lift" and asking "whether sales would have happened anyway." — [Agency Reporter, 28 Apr 2026](https://agencyreporter.com/attribution-broken-indian-marketers-doing-about-it/)
- **Inc42 editorial (unattributed block quote).** "ROAS has become more of a diagnostic tool to judge whether a specific ad or creative is working". Also, the founder of TruCommerce (vendor) says consumers get recommendations "without even visiting a brand's website", which erodes attribution. — [Inc42, 21 Jul 2026](https://inc42.com/features/the-end-of-the-roas-era-in-d2c/)
- **Kae Capital [PARAPHRASE].** 3X ROAS vs about 5% true margin: platform metrics overstate economics. — same source.
- **Agency and vendor blogs (vendor-interested, unnamed authors).**
  - A WhatsApp incrementality write-up claims recent buyers ordered the same with or without a message, with lift concentrated in loyal customers lapsed 4-8 months. It warns that a Meta-capped comparison group "isn't random" and overstates lift. — [Smacient](https://smacient.com/whatsapp-incrementality-claude-skill/)
  - An Indian benchmark blog advises holding out a random slice of a WhatsApp broadcast quarterly to measure true incremental revenue. — [theshizz.in](https://theshizz.in/blog/email-whatsapp-marketing-benchmarks-india)
  - Balistro claims a 30-60% gap between last-click ROAS and incremental ROAS. This is an agency figure. — [Balistro, Jul 2026](https://www.balistro.com/blog/marketing-attribution-in-2026)

### Inferences
- Holdout or incrementality measurement for retention campaigns is a whitespace in the public Indian discourse. Paid-media incrementality is discussed. Owned-channel (WhatsApp, email) incrementality is discussed only by small agencies and tool builders. This supports a positioning angle, but it also means there is little named merchant demand to cite.
- The Smacient finding (no lift on recent buyers, lift on lapsed loyalists) is directionally the core logic of a repeat-purchase decision agent. It comes from a small vendor and should be presented as a practitioner observation, not as evidence.

### Gaps
- No quote found from CleverTap, MoEngage, WebEngage, Netcore, BiteSpeed, GoKwik (KwikEngage) or LimeChat leaders on control groups or holdouts. Their product docs likely describe control-group features, but these were not fetched.
- No published Indian lift study for WhatsApp or email retention campaigns was found.

## Q4. Who says acquisition or CAC is still the main bottleneck?

### Takeaway
Several voices frame acquisition cost, conversion or RTO as the binding constraint:
- Beco (home care) says acquisition and engagement, not repeat, is the problem.
- Renee still spends about 45% of revenue on marketing.
- Neeru's highlights sale-event acquisition.
- GoKwik's founding thesis (Chirag Taneja, paraphrased) centred on COD conversion, RTO and rising acquisition costs.
- Unicommerce's platform data argues operations (RTO and COD) matter more than marketing spend.

Rising CAC is widely asserted. Hard numbers come mostly from agency blogs.

### Cited Findings
- **Aditya Ruia, Beco.** "In categories like home care, repeat isn't the problem – brand engagement and acquisition is." Verdict: Contradicts. — [Inc42, 9 Apr 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/mumbai-edition-of-d2cx-converge-decodes-the-%E2%82%B9100-cr-playbook-for-d2c-brands/)
- **Ashutosh Valani, Renee [PARAPHRASE].** Marketing spend is about 45% of revenue, against an expected sub-40%. — [Inc42, 21 Jul 2026](https://inc42.com/features/the-end-of-the-roas-era-in-d2c/)
- **Saurav Agarwal, PromotEdge (agency) [PARAPHRASE].** New brands put about 95% of marketing into paid or inorganic channels, shifting toward 80:20 or 70:30 over time. Vendor. — same source.
- **Chirag Taneja, GoKwik [PARAPHRASE].** As former CRO of Bombay Shaving Company he saw low COD conversion, RTO orders and rising acquisition costs as industry-wide problems. GoKwik claims its checkout reduces CAC (company claim, no figures). Vendor. Verdict: Contradicts-leaning (bottleneck is conversion and RTO). — [YourStory 2021](https://yourstory.com/2021/11/startup-solving-buying-experience-customers-d2c-brands-ecommerce/amp)
- **GoKwik data (vendor).**
  - Average Indian e-commerce RTO is about 20-25%, rising to about 40% by category or location (internal data cited at a YourStory D2C Carwaan event, Sept 2024). The page redirected and was not fetched, so this was seen via search summary only. — [YourStory](https://ts.yourstory.com/2024/09/d2c-carwaan-surat-edition-founders-explore-return-to-origin-rates-root-cause)
  - GoKwik claims its merchants avoided up to Rs 130 Cr of RTO losses in 2023. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/business/gokwik-helps-ecommerce-brands-save-over-130-cr-worth-of-rto-losses-in-2023)
- **BiteSpeed x Razorpay release (vendor).** Narration notes that customer acquisition costs are rising, without quantifying. — [Tribune, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/bitespeed-partners-with-razorpay-to-launch-unified-ai-conversion-and-retention-stack-for-d2c-brands-2-2/)
- **Rising-CAC figures (agency and vendor blogs, unverified).**
  - Meta CAC up 25-40% over three years. — [Adtitude Media](https://www.adtitudemedia.com/blog/rising-cac-platform-dependency-d2c-profitability)
  - About ₹380 to ₹502 (2025 to 2026, +32%). — [Growwwtech](https://growwwtech.com/blog/state-of-indian-d2c-2026-industry-report)

### Inferences
- The contradicting view is less "CAC is the bottleneck" and more "the margin leak is upstream", in RTO, COD, returns and conversion. That is GoKwik's and Unicommerce's thesis and The Pant Project's experience. A retention-first pitch should acknowledge that, for many Indian merchants, RTO and returns are a bigger, more measurable leak than missed repeat orders.
- High marketing-to-revenue ratios at scaled brands (Renee) suggest acquisition cost remains the largest line item. "Profitable retention" is therefore partly a response to CAC pain rather than a separate problem.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 named in-house performance-marketing head was quoted on CAC as the main bottleneck. Practitioner sources were agency blogs.
- The exchange4media 2023 piece "Digital CAC on the rise" (which per search summary contains founder views on shifting from acquisition cost to existing customers, and a "repeat rate × AOV × orders per 100 new customers" heuristic) returned 403. Names and quotes were not captured. — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/digital-cac-on-the-rise-how-are-d2c-brands-keeping-up-132495.html)

### Takeaway
I found no true merchant-priority survey from GoKwik, Shiprocket, BiteSpeed or Shopify India in 2023-2026. What exists is platform or transaction data (Unicommerce, GoKwik), market-sizing reports (Shiprocket-CII-Praxis) and self-reported vendor claims. Repeat-rate "benchmarks" circulating online (10-30%, 18.8%, 20-30%) mostly lack primary sources.

  - Method: built from "410 million real shipments and transaction data across 6,000+ brands". The page says "No surveys. No estimates." This is platform data, not a survey.
  - RTO: 39.2% at the Nov 2025 festive peak, 25.6% in Jan 2026, 21.0% in Mar 2026 for brands running the fixes.
  - Returns: COD orders returned at 58% during the festive quarter vs under 15% for prepaid.
  - Repeat: a 90-day repeat rate "below 20%" is flagged as a warning sign. No measured repeat rate is given.
  - CAC: not covered.
  - Positioning: argues operations matter more than marketing spend.

### Inferences
- No ecosystem player publicly surveys merchants on "acquisition vs retention" priorities, so a claim that "merchants say retention is their top problem" cannot currently be backed by a vendor survey. The closest hard data (Unicommerce, GoKwik) actually points at RTO and COD as the measurable leak.
- The pitch should present repeat-rate benchmarks as ranges from unverified secondary sources, or replace them with the challenge sponsor's own data.

### Gaps
- GoKwik, Shiprocket, BiteSpeed and Shopify India report or PDF pages were not located within budget. GoKwik's and Shiprocket's own report hubs, and Shiprocket's 2026 IPO DRHP (which may contain merchant-cohort and retention data), should be checked directly.
- MoEngage, CleverTap and WebEngage India benchmark reports were referenced by aggregators but not opened.
